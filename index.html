How a node decides

Read

Python polls DHT22, HC-SR04, SW-420 over GPIO, and soil moisture and MQ-2 gas through the ADS1115 over I2C.

See

The USB camera feeds OpenCV, then a quantized YOLOv8n model runs on the Pi CPU at about 5 FPS.

Agree

High heat AND high gas AND smoke seen on camera. If only one is true, the node stays on watch.

Warn

The local buzzer sounds and the event is logged. Only critical alerts and summaries go to the cloud.

Rules used in this demo

  • Fire: temp above 45 °C, gas above 400 ppm, camera smoke confidence above 60%.
  • Flood: water below 40 cm from sensor and soil above 85%.
  • Landslide: soil above 90% and vibration detected.
  • Any single rule met puts the node on watch.

Values on this page are simulated to show the interface. Thresholds are examples to be tuned per site.

What changes on the ground

  • Floods, landslides and wildfires are seen in their early stages.
  • Local warnings skip the round trip to a distant server.
  • Cross-checking sensors with vision lowers false alarms.
  • Core monitoring keeps running without internet.
  • Solar nodes cover terrain that is hard to reach.

Who uses it

  • State disaster management authorities and forest departments
  • Municipalities and industries
  • Vulnerable communities, through prioritized warnings

Revenue: node deployment, installation, annual maintenance, and monitoring or analytics services. Start from one pilot site, then grow to district and state networks.
